Zümrütevler Square

A public square in Maltepe, Istanbul, redrawn from the height of a three year old. The work began from the question the Bernard van Leer Foundation puts to cities through its Urban95 programme: what would you do differently if you saw the city from 95 centimetres?

From there a broken pavement is a barrier, traffic is a wall of noise, a bench is out of reach and a planter is the horizon. The team used paint, planters and movable furniture to test the changes cheaply before any permanent construction was justified.

Interim, then permanent The interim implementation was realised in October 2019. After a season of observation the permanent square was completed in August 2020. 1,075 m² of road was turned over to pedestrians.

What was measured People spending time in the square rose by 72%. On weekday afternoons there were three and a half times as many babies and small children. Children walking alone rose by 43%, and 89% of them walked inside the marked area.

Yalı Square and Bike Path

The same method, one neighbourhood along the Marmara coast. Irregular parking in front of a primary school was removed to make a neighbourhood square, Onurlu Street was tested as a pedestrian priority street, and a bike path was planned from the metro station to the parks on the coast.

In phases A pop-up trial in September 2021 tested the street. On the strength of the response, a six month interim implementation followed in April 2022.

Tree of Life

An immersive installation. The client said: “I want a tree that feels alive.” That sentence was the entire brief. Everything else had to be directed.

Seven iterations Each one removed something. The first brief asked for a spectacular tree at maximum particle density. Review four gave the trunk weight below the first fork. Review seven took away 40% of the leaf particles so the canopy could breathe.

The final version was quieter, slower and more alive than the first. The client had asked for spectacle. What they received was presence. They approved it in silence.

About

A city, a building, a room, an object, a screen. I have never experienced these as separate disciplines. They are different magnifications of the same problem.

What changes between scales is the material. What remains constant is the method: observe, question, give form. Seven years of projects, four countries, four orders of magnitude.
