What would you do differently if you saw the city from 95 centimetres?

Elif Ebrar Zelka is an architect in Split. A city, a building, a room, an object and a screen are one problem at different magnifications. Change the scale and watch a three year old appear.

95 centimetres

The height of a three year old, and the true length of this red line on your screen.

Zümrütevler Square, Istanbul Maltepe

The question comes from the Bernard van Leer Foundation’s Urban95 programme. At Zümrütevler it was asked of a real square, with Superpool, Maltepe Municipality and NACTO GDCI.

Broken pavements become barriers.

Traffic becomes a wall of noise.

A bench is unreachable.

A planter is a horizon.

The team used paint, planters and movable furniture to test the changes before any permanent construction. The interim was realised in October 2019; the permanent square was completed in August 2020, with 1,075 m² of road turned over to pedestrians.

After the intervention the square was no longer a transit corridor. It had become a destination.

Tree of Life

The client said: “I want a tree that feels alive.” That sentence was the entire brief. Everything else had to be directed.

Seven iterations. Each one removed something. The final version was quieter, slower and more alive than the first. The client had asked for spectacle. What they received was presence. They approved it in silence.

  1. Iteration 1

    “A spectacular generative tree. Maximum particle density. The audience should feel overwhelmed.”

  2. Review 4

    “The trunk needs weight. Right now it floats. Add 30% more branch thickness below the first fork.”

  3. Review 7

    “The tree is suffocating. Reduce leaf particles by 40%. Every particle that remains must earn its place.”

  4. Approved

    “The tree grows, responds, breathes light. The emotion is quiet wonder, not spectacle.”
